\prefacesection{Abstract}
Modern systems are increasingly realized as systems of systems, where independently managed constituent systems collaborate to achieve shared goals. In parallel, digital twins are used to monitor and optimize system behaviour. When digitally twinned systems are composed according to system of systems principles, a new class of systems emerges. In these systems, maintaining reliable behaviour becomes challenging as systems join, leave, or behave unexpectedly. These disturbances can be understood across two dimensions: whether a system is actively participating and how well it is functioning. In this thesis, I propose a model-driven approach to manage disturbances in systems of twinned systems. At its core, a statechart model captures two dimensions of the state of constituent systems---their health, representing their ability to operate correctly, and their decision to belong to the system---as orthogonal regions and defines how they relate to each other. The model specifies how constituents join, contribute, and withdraw based on their health and their autonomous decisions about belonging to the system. This provides a structured mechanism for managing disturbances at the system level, improving reliability by progressively restricting undesirable states. To demonstrate the utility of the approach, I implement a case study where it is applied to a complex event processing service within a system of systems.
