\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis investigated a new class of systems, systems of twinned systems, where multiple digital twins interact to achieve capabilities beyond individual systems. The systematic literature review conducted in this work shows that these systems introduce unique reliability challenges due to dynamic system compositions and interdependencies between constituents. These factors lead to disturbances that propagate across the system and degrade overall behaviour. To limit the impact of these disturbances, this thesis proposed a model-driven approach that represents the relationship between constituent health and participation using lifecycle statecharts. These models define and enforce coordination behaviour at the system level, ensuring contributions remain aligned with each constituent's operational condition. The demonstrated architecture and evaluation showed that this approach improves reliability of complex event processing by enabling controlled participation and compensating for missing or degraded contributions under uncertainty. Overall, this work establishes a foundation for managing reliability in systems of twinned systems and positions lifecycle models as enforceable contracts for coordinating adaptive system behaviour.
