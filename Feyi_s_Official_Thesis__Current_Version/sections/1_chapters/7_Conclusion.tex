\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

Systems of twinned systems represent a new class of systems where multiple digital twins are composed according to system of systems principles. These systems achieve capabilities beyond those of individual systems. The systematic literature review shows that these systems experience reliability challenges due to dynamic composition and interdependencies between constituents. These challenges lead to disturbances that propagate across the system and affect overall behaviour. This thesis proposes a model-driven approach that captures the relationship between constituent health, representing operational condition, and belonging, representing the decision to participate in the system, using statecharts. The approach uses this model to define reliability levels that manage how constituents operate within the system. This prevents configurations that introduce disturbances. The approach is realized in a CEP-based middleware. The demonstration shows that controlling when and how constituents contribute, together with compensation, maintains continuous event streams under data loss and improves pattern detection compared to uncompensated execution. This shifts disturbance handling to the system level. Overall, the proposed approach provides a structured way to manage reliability in systems of twinned systems.
