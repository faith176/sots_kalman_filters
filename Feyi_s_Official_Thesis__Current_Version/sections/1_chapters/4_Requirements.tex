As discussed in \secref{sec:background-cep}, complex event processing  correlates events, detects temporal patterns, and extracts higher-level insights from heterogeneous sources—capabilities well aligned with the operational needs of SoTS. Its event-driven paradigm supports the monitoring, coordination, and control of large-scale, dynamically interacting systems.

However, the systematic study (\chref{ch:literature-review}) indicates that current SoTS implementations lack mechanisms for reliable operation, limiting their ability to integrate CEP. This issue arises at two levels. At the sensing layer, missing or unreliable data, latency, and communication loss degrade event accuracy and decision dependability. At the architectural level, dynamic reconfiguration and runtime changes in system composition challenge stability and consistent data propagation.

CEP systems are also highly sensitive to input quality; even minor uncertainty in event timing or sensor integrity can propagate through correlation rules, causing false or missed detections. Despite existing mitigation strategies, uncertainty persists due to dynamic composition, emergent behaviour, and external disruptions such as packet loss and sensor degradation. Reliability is further challenged by the heterogeneity and interdependencies of SoS, where local faults can cascade and disrupt global performance~\cite{ed2018deterministic}. Classical reliability methods are therefore insufficient~\cite{bukowski2016system}. From a resilience perspective, when prevention is impossible, systems must adapt to maintain acceptable performance under unexpected conditions~\cite{madni2020constructing}. Thus, dependable operation requires mechanisms that can compensate for imperfect data rather than relying solely on fault elimination.


The Uncertainty in CEP Taxonomy~\cite{wasserkrug2006taxonomy} (See \secref{subsec:background-cep-uncertainty}) distinguishes between two principal dimensions:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep, topsep=0pt]
\item origin uncertainty, arising from noise, incompleteness, or inconsistency in observed streams; and
\item Element uncertainty, arising from ambiguity in event patterns, thresholds, or decision logic.
\end{itemize}
This work focuses on the origin uncertainty quadrant, where the reliability of observed data directly determines the correctness of event detection. 

The remainder of this chapter derives a set of requirements for a Reliability-Enhanced Complex Event Processing framework that uses compensation mechanisms to ensure dependable behaviour in SoTS. These requirements are systematically grounded in the empirical insights of the systematic study and form the conceptual and architectural foundation for the framework introduced in Chapter~\ref{ch:architecture}.
