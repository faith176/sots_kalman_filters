\section{Results}\label{sec:literature-review-results}
We present the findings derived from the systematic mapping study.

%---------------------------------------------
\subsection{Motivations for Combining DTs and SoS}\label{subsec:literature-review-results-motivations}
The motivations extracted from the primary studies fall into four categories: optimization, integration, validation, and maintainability, summarized in \tabref{tab:motivations-table}. Optimization is the most common driver (\xofyp{30}{80}), typically aiming to improve coordination and cross-system decision-making, such as reducing turnaround time in UAV–USV landing operations \cite{li2022cognitive}. Integration (\xofyp{25}{80}) focuses on unifying heterogeneous DTs to support coordinated management in domains like energy and manufacturing \cite{monsalve2021novel}. Validation studies (\xofyp{15}{80}) use SoTS to test multi-agent behaviours in controlled environments, including vehicle coordination \cite{dahmen2022modeling}. Maintainability (\xofyp{10}{80}) emphasizes predictive diagnostics and lifecycle monitoring. Overall, SoTS are pursued to extend the analytical precision and real-time awareness of DTs across distributed systems.

\subsection{Architectural Structures in SoTS}\label{subsec:literature-review-results-architecture}
To understand how DTs and SoS are combined, studies were classified using the SoTS framework from \secref{sec:literature-review-classification}. As shown in \tabref{tab:sots-type-table}, Acknowledged SoTS are most prevalent (\xofyp{31}{80}), pairing distributed autonomy with a coordinating DT. For example grid-scale Digital Twin Masters synchronizing local clients \cite{monsalve2021novel}. Directed SoTS (\xofyp{26}{80}) use a controlling DT to enforce shared objectives, common in infrastructure and factory settings \cite{reiche2021digital, li2024comprehensive}. Collaborative SoTS (\xofyp{19}{80}) use peer-to-peer coordination, such as DT agents negotiating production tasks \cite{vogel-heuser2021approach}. Virtual SoTS (\xofyp{4}{80}) remain emerging, explored in adaptive cyber-physical collectives \cite{esterle2021digital}. Overall, most implementations rely on centralized or semi-centralized coordination rather than fully decentralized forms.

\subsection{SoS Dimensions Represented in SoTS}\label{subsec:literature-review-results-sos-characteristics}

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1.05\linewidth]{figures/Literature Review/rq4/sosDimensions.pdf}
    \caption{Prevalence of SoS dimensions across SoTS studies.}
    \label{fig:sos-dim}
\end{figure}

As shown in \figref{fig:sos-dim}, distribution (92.5\%) and independence (88.75\%) are the most consistently supported dimensions, reflecting SoTS’ focus on geographically dispersed and managerially independent constituents. Interoperability (76.25\%) and interdependence (77.5\%) are also strongly represented due to cross-DT data flows and exchange. In contrast, autonomy (47.5\%) and emergence (53.75\%) appear only partially, and dynamic dimensions, reconfiguration (43.75\%) and evolution (37.5\%), are least addressed. This indicates that while structural coordination is widely supported, runtime adaptation and long-term SoS behaviour remain underdeveloped.

\subsection{Reliability as a Non-Functional Property}\label{subsec:literature-review-results-reliability}
\input{tables/Literature Review/RQs/rq5/reliabilityTable}
Reliability was addressed in \xofyp{41}{80} studies but rarely beyond architectural mechanisms. As summarized in \tabref{tab:reliability-table}, common strategies include fallback to local twins during communication loss \cite{alam2017c2ps, larsen2024towards}, asynchronous messaging to tolerate intermittent connectivity \cite{acharya2023twins, liu2020web-based}, and recalibration loops for restoring synchronization \cite{esterle2021digital, villalonga2021decision-making}. However, only two studies formally modeled reliability, and just three validated it through fault injection or simulation \cite{park2020digital, saraeian2022digital}.

\subsection{Technical Maturity of SoTS Research}
\label{subsec:literature-review-results-trl}

To assess the maturity of current SoTS work, each study was classified using 
Technology Readiness Levels (TRLs)~\cite{mankins2009technology}, grouped into 
five clusters ranging from early concept exploration (TRL~1–2) to fully 
operational systems (TRL~9).

\input{tables/Literature Review/RQs/rq6/trlTable}

As shown in \tabref{tab:trl-table}, most SoTS research remains at low to mid-level maturity: demonstration prototypes dominate, followed by early-stage concept studies, with only a single work reaching operational deployment. This indicates that current SoTS implementations are largely validated in controlled or simulated environments rather than real operational settings.


%---------------------------------------------
\subsection{Adoption of Standards}\label{subsec:literature-review-results-standards}
\input{tables/Literature Review/RQs/rq6/standards}

Standard adoption remains uneven (\tabref{tab:standards-table}). OPC~UA is most frequently used (\xofyp{13}{80}) for secure, interoperable communication among DTs \cite{dobie2024network, joseph2021aggregated}. The Asset Administration Shell (IEC~63278) appears in (\xofyp{8}{80}) studies to provide structured asset representation \cite{gill2022method, gollner2022collaborative}, while RAMI~4.0 supports layered architectural alignment \cite{binder2021utilizing}. Domain-specific standards such as VANET, IPv6, ISO/IEC/IEEE~15288, ISA-95, and IEC~61850 appear sporadically, and security-oriented standards (e.g., GDPR, OAuth~2.0) are rare. Most studies reference standards as guidance rather than strict compliance frameworks, reflecting the early-stage maturity of SoTS practice.
