SoTS emerge from the intersection of SoS and DTs. SoS research focuses on coordinating autonomous, heterogeneous systems to achieve collective goals~\cite{maier1998architecting}, while DTs provide continuously synchronized digital representations of physical assets capable of real-time monitoring, prediction, and control~\cite{kritzinger2018digital, tao2018digital}. Integrating these two ideas enables new forms of coordination: DTs offer SoS stronger situational awareness and predictive capability, while SoS provide DTs a scalable, multi-system context in which collective behaviour can emerge.

\paragraph{Prior work already exhibits this convergence in practice.}  
Smart-city and cyber–physical mobility systems provide some of the most prominent examples of what can be interpreted as SoTS. Studies describe how multiple DTs can be hierarchically integrated to support coordination across large-scale SoS, particularly in urban environments. City-level DT architectures aggregate infrastructure-, vehicle-, and service-level twins to enable monitoring, analysis, and optimization across interconnected urban subsystems \cite{tao2019digital,schroeder2021digital,gill2024toward}. Within these environments, constituent systems are typically autonomous and may dynamically join or leave coordinated activities. Several works emphasize self-organization and self-optimization mechanisms, where local DT-enabled decisions influence emergent system-level behaviour \cite{esterle2021digital,ghanbarifard2023digital}. Autonomous vehicle platooning in urban traffic illustrates this pattern, with coordination emerging through loosely coupled DT-to-DT interactions \cite{borth2019digital,olsson2023systems}.

It synthesizes findings relevant to this thesis and introduces a SoTS classification framework (\secref{sec:literature-review-classification}) to structure and compare architectural patterns in existing work. The results are presented in \secref{sec:literature-review-results} and summarized through key takeaways.
