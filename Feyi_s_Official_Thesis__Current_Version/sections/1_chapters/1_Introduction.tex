\chapter{Introduction}\label{ch:introduction}

System complexity is increasing as systems engineering increasingly relies on digitally integrated and autonomous systems operating across expanding scopes and organizational boundaries \citep{incose2035}. Many contemporary systems no longer operate in isolation, but instead cooperate with independently managed systems to support shared objectives. In this setting, system of systems (SoS) describe configurations in which autonomous constituent systems coordinate their behaviour while retaining operational and managerial independence \citep{maier1998architecting}. SoS engineering emphasizes loose coupling, flexible coordination, and continuous evolution, allowing constituent systems to join, leave, or reorganize as objectives and operating conditions change, and enabling collective behaviours that cannot be achieved by individual systems alone \citep{nielsen2015systems}.

In parallel, digital twins (DTs) have emerged as a means of achieving tight integration between physical systems and their digital representations. A DT maintains a bidirectional, continuously synchronized relationship with the system it represents, allowing observations from the physical world to update digital models and enabling analyses performed in the digital domain to influence physical operation \citep{kritzinger2018digital}. Through this digital--physical convergence, DTs support monitoring, simulation, prediction, and decision support across the system lifecycle \citep{pires2019digital,zhang2024digital,leng2021digital,david2024infonomics}.

When digitally twinned systems are organized according to SoS principles, a new class of systems emerges. These systems combine the autonomy and flexible coordination of SoS with the continuous digital--physical alignment provided by DTs, giving rise to what this thesis refers to as \emph{systems of twinned systems (SoTS)} \cite{adesanya2025systems}.

\begin{definitionframe}
Systems of twinned systems (SoTS) comprise of digitally twinned systems, i.e., digital and physical twins, organized by system of systems principles, in which digitally twinned systems may act as autonomous constituents and collaborate to achieve complex goals.
\end{definitionframe}

\paragraph{On reliability in SoTS}
Reliability challenges in SoTS arise from disturbances affecting the behaviour of constituent systems \cite{ferreira2023towards}. Because constituents operate autonomously while participating in coordinated activities, system structure and behaviour may evolve during operation: systems may join or withdraw, and participating systems may degrade or behave unpredictably. These dynamics can disrupt the coordination mechanisms through which system-level capabilities emerge.  Observations from SoTS research suggest that such disturbances commonly manifest along two aspects of constituent behaviour: whether a system participates in the SoTS and how reliably it operates while participating. Changes in participation may remove required capabilities, while operational degradation may introduce incomplete or unreliable contributions that affect system-level reasoning. These observations motivate the need for a structured way to characterize and reason about disturbances in SoTS.

\paragraph{Thesis focus.}
This thesis addresses this need by characterizing SoTS through a systematic analysis of existing literature, identifying reliability as a recurring but insufficiently addressed concern. It then introduces a conceptual model of disturbances based on two observable dimensions of constituent state: belonging, representing participation, and health, representing operational condition. Building on this model, the thesis develops a state-based reliability framework in which belonging and health evolve concurrently and are represented using executable Statecharts that regulate how constituents join, participate, degrade, and withdraw. The approach is demonstrated through a proof-of-concept implementation in which lifecycle statecharts coordinate constituent behaviour within a complex event processing (CEP) \cite{cugola2015complex} architecture.

\section*{Research Contributions}
This thesis advances the understanding and operation of SoTS through the following contributions:

\textbf{Contribution 1 - Characterizing Systems of Twinned Systems and Their Reliability Gaps (\chref{ch:literature-review})} \\
Presents a systematic analysis of existing research to characterize SoTS as a distinct class emerging from the integration of DTs and SoS principles. The analysis identifies key structural and behavioural properties, and reveals a gap in current work: while reliability is recognized as important, explicit mechanisms to address it are largely absent.

\textbf{Contribution 2 - A Conceptual Model of Disturbances and Reliability in SoTS (\chref{ch:reliable-sots})} \\
Introduces a state-based model of disturbances in SoTS using two dimensions of constituent state: belonging (participation) and health (operational condition). These dimensions form the basis of a Statechart-based reliability framework that manages how constituents join, participate, degrade, and withdraw.

\textbf{Contribution 3 - Architecture and Demonstration of Statechart-Based SoTS Coordination (\chref{ch:demonstration})} \\
Demonstrates how the proposed reliability framework is operationalized in a CEP-based SoTS architecture, where executable lifecycle statecharts coordinate constituent behaviour. The implementation integrates event-driven processing with compensation mechanisms to support reliable system-level reasoning under degradation.

\begin{runningexample}{Running Example: CEP-Based SoTS Scenario}

Throughout the thesis, concepts are illustrated using a running example consisting of a SoTS composed of DTs corresponding to physical assets. Each DT monitors its associated asset and produces timestamped observations, which are translated into events and published to a shared event stream.

A CEP layer (see \secref{sec:background-cep}) ingests these streams and evaluates patterns across events from multiple DTs to detect higher-level system situations. When such situations are identified, the CEP layer may emit coordination signals or alerts that influence constituent behaviour.

\end{runningexample}



\vspace{1em}
Together, these contributions establish a foundation for reasoning about reliability in SoTS by framing reliability challenges in terms of disturbances affecting constituent participation and operational condition, and by demonstrating how architectural coordination mechanisms manage these dynamics during operation. The remainder of the thesis builds on this foundation by first presenting relevant background and related work (\chref{ch:background}, \chref{ch:literature-review}). It then introduces the conceptual disturbance model and state-based reliability framework for SoTS (\chref{ch:reliable-sots}), followed by a realization of the approach in a CEP-based system architecture and its demonstration through a proof-of-concept implementation (\chref{ch:demonstration}). The thesis concludes with a discussion of limitations, broader implications, and directions for future research (\chref{ch:discussion}, \chref{ch:conclusion}).
