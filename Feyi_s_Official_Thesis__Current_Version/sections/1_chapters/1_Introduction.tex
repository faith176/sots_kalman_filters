\chapter{Introduction}\label{ch:introduction}
The growing interconnection of systems and the drive toward autonomous operation are contributing to a sharp rise in system complexity \cite{incose2035}, both across and within individual systems. At the system level, independent systems are increasingly engineered to cooperate and achieve objectives that exceed their individual capabilities. Such arrangements are described as system of systems (SoS). SoS are large-scale, distributed collections of independently developed and managed systems that collaborate to fulfill higher-level goals while retaining operational independence \cite{maier1998architecting}. SoS evolve dynamically as their constituents join, leave, or reorganize in response to changing objectives, giving rise to emergent behaviours that cannot be achieved by individual systems alone \cite{nielsen2015systems}.
Within each constituent system, internal complexity is also increasing as physical systems are integrated with digital layers that continuously measure, compute, and adjust system behaviour. Digital twins (DTs) represent this integration. A DT is a digital representation of a physical system that mirrors its real-time behaviour and state~\cite{kritzinger2018digital}. Through continuous data exchange between the physical and digital domains, DTs maintain awareness of system conditions \cite{pires2019digital,zhang2024digital}. This information enables analysis, in which models or algorithms interpret observed behaviour to estimate current performance or predict future states. DTs also support adaptation, where insights obtained from the digital representation are used to guide control actions or adjustments in the physical system \cite{leng2021digital}. As digital twins become more common, they enable coordinated monitoring and control among physical systems through their digital representations.

The convergence of system-level coordination found in systems of systems and the internal digital representation enabled by digital twins gives rise to a new class of systems referred to in this thesis as \textbf{systems of twinned systems (SoTS)}, which can be formally described as follows:

\begin{definitionframe}
A system of twinned systems comprises digitally twinned systems organized according to system-of-systems principles, in which digital twins act as autonomous constituents that collaborate to achieve collective goals.
\end{definitionframe}

SoTS extend the principles of SoS coordination into the digital domain. Through their digital twins, constituent systems can be composed and coordinated virtually while maintaining control over their physical counterparts. This configuration enables collective behaviour to emerge without additional physical integration between systems. This capability supports coordinated adaptation and shared awareness across interconnected, heterogeneous environments.

SoTS operate through the continuous exchange of information among constituent twins. This coordination enables collective behaviour but also introduces peroids of temporary unavailability that challenge reliable operation. Reliability, as defined by \citet{avizienis2004basic}, depends not only on the correctness of behaviour but also on the sustained continuity of service. SoTS face this challenge on two fronts, as they inherit reliability concerns from both SoS and DTs. Because constituent twins operate autonomously, temporary disconnections, communication delays, or changes in inter-system coordination can interrupt data flow and disrupt analysis, giving rise to reliability challenges across multiple layers of the system. At the sensing and communication layers, latency, drift, and intermittent faults are common and can compromise data availability \cite{belgacem2025toward}. At the system level, dynamic reconfiguration or re-tasking of constituent systems can alter information exchange patterns and timing, leading to inconsistencies in collective behaviour \cite{fang2020improving}. Without compensation mechanisms, such disruptions can propagate through the system and undermine coordinated operation \cite{hanmer2013patterns}

These limitations motivate the central question of this thesis:
\textit{How can a system of twinned systems maintain reliable operation when data from constituent systems becomes temporarily unavailable?}

Addressing this challenge requires an approach that allows SoTS to continue operating, even when constituent data becomes temporarily unavailable. This research develops an architecture that compensates for data disruptions by reconstructing missing information using state-based estimation and by quantifying the uncertainty associated with each prediction. The resulting data streams contain both observed and reconstructed inputs, allowing downstream modules to reason with awareness of uncertainty, defer actions when confidence is low, or gracefully degrade until normal operation resumes.

The architecture is demonstrated within a complex event processing (CEP) pipeline, which serves as a reasoning mechanism for continuously interpreting event streams to detect meaningful patterns and collective behaviours among the constituents of SoTS. This thesis considers SoTS as event-driven systems because their operation depends on the continuous generation, exchange, and interpretation of events among constituent twins. In this context, an event refers to a discrete observation or state change detected within a constituent system, such as a new sensor reading \cite{cugola2012processing}. CEP is a technique for analyzing sequences of such atomic events to infer higher-level situations that reflect emergent behaviour \cite{luckham2002power}. Most existing CEP implementations, however, assume complete and uninterrupted input streams. Prior research has shown that explicitly handling uncertainty within event reasoning is essential for enabling automated and timely responses under real-world conditions \cite{wasserkrug2006taxonomy}. This implementation provides a controlled environment for evaluating how the proposed architecture responds to temporary data unavailability within event processing. Experiments simulate temporary data loss and system reconfiguration to measure reconstruction accuracy and its effect on event detection, with results compared against a baseline configuration.

\section*{Research Contributions}

This thesis advances the reliable operation of systems of twinned systems through four main contributions that collectively define, analyze, design, and evaluate an implemented solution.

\textbf{Contribution 1 – Characterizing the Class of Systems of Twinned Systems (Chapter~\ref{ch:background})}
Introduces SoTS as a distinct class that extends SoS through DTs, where the composition of virtualized constituents enables flexible coordination among distributed physical systems.
The definition establishes a conceptual and structural foundation for understanding the organization, composition, and behaviour of SoTS.

\textbf{Contribution 2 – Analyzing the State of the Art in Systems of Twinned Systems (Chapter~\ref{ch:literature-review})}
Conducts a systematic study of the state of the art in SoTS, encompassing their conceptual foundations, architectural approaches, and methods of development. The synthesis consolidates current knowledge on how these systems are structured and operated, emphasizing the role of reliable operation in SoTS environments.

\textbf{Contribution 3 – Creating an Architecture for Reliable Operation in Systems of Twinned Systems (Chapter~\ref{ch:architecture})}
Develops an architecture that supports reliable operation in SoTS by enabling continued functioning when data from constituent systems becomes temporarily unavailable.
The architecture introduces a preprocessing component that detects data gaps, reconstructs missing values through state-based estimation, and assigns confidence indicators to express uncertainty in reconstructed data.


\textbf{Contribution 4 – Evaluating the Reliable SoTS Architecture within a Complex Event Processing Pipeline (Chapter~\ref{ch:results})}
Implements and integrates the proposed architecture within a complex event processing pipeline to evaluate its performance under periods of temporary data unavailability. Experiments simulate constituent disconnections representative of reconfiguration behaviour in SoTS, with performance assessed through reconstruction accuracy and event-detection correctness relative to a baseline configuration.
