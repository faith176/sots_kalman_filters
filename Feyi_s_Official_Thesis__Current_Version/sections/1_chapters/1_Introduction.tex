\chapter{Introduction}\label{ch:introduction}
The growing interconnection of systems and the increasing use of automation are contributing to a sharp rise in system complexity \cite{incose2035}, both across and within individual systems. At a system level, independently developed systems are increasingly engineered to cooperate so they can achieve objectives that exceed their individual capabilities. Such arrangements are described as systems of systems (SoS): large, distributed collections of autonomous systems that collaborate toward higher-level goals while retaining independent operation \cite{maier1998architecting}. Because SoS constituents join, leave, or reorganize in response to changing objectives, their collective behaviour evolves over time and gives rise to outcomes that individual systems cannot achieve alone \cite{nielsen2015systems}.

Within each constituent system, internal complexity is also increasing as modern software systems collect, interpret, and act on streams of events. Many contemporary applications operate according to event semantics: they update internal state, detect meaningful changes, and trigger higher-level behaviour based on the arrival of discrete observations over time \cite{cugola2012processing}. Digital twins (DTs) represent a prominent example of this. A DT is a digital representation of a system that mirrors its real-time state through continuous data exchange \cite{kritzinger2018digital}. This connection enables awareness of current conditions \cite{pires2019digital,zhang2024digital}, supports analysis through models that interpret or forecast behaviour, and provides services such as optimization and simulation-based decision support \cite{leng2021digital}.

The convergence of system-level coordination in SoS with the real-time digital representations provided by DTs gives rise to a new class of systems referred to in this thesis as \textbf{systems of twinned systems (SoTS)}:

\begin{definitionframe}
A system of twinned systems comprises digitally twinned systems organized according to system-of-systems principles, in which digital twins act as autonomous constituents that collaborate to achieve collective goals.
\end{definitionframe}

SoTS extend SoS coordination into the digital domain. Through their DTs, systems can be composed and coordinated virtually while still maintaining control over their physical counterparts. This supports distributed monitoring and decision-making without requiring tight physical integration.

A defining characteristic of SoTS is their reliance on continuous information exchange among their DTs. Their operation depends on consuming timely event streams, yet the structural and behavioural properties of SoTS make continuous data availability difficult to guarantee. Constituents may join, leave, or change roles; communication paths may shift; sensing quality can fluctuate; and network or platform conditions may vary over time. These forms of dynamism arise from the autonomy, independence, heterogeneity, and distribution that define SoS and, by extension, SoTS. Although well understood individually, their combined effect on data continuity is often overlooked. In practice, these behaviours create temporary gaps in event streams that degrade the quality of the information available to DTs and the services they are meant to provide.

\paragraph{On the reliability of SoTS.}

Reliability, as defined by \citet{avizienis2004basic}, requires both correctness and continuity of services. In SoTS, this expectation applies not only to the physical constituents but also to the DTs that provide higher-level functions such as simulation-based optimization and other analytic services. These capabilities depend on a steady flow of observational events to keep the DT’s representation of its system aligned with reality. When SoTS reconfigurations or other disruptions interrupt this flow, the DT loses the evidence it needs to remain synchronized, and the quality of the services it provides begins to degrade. When dealing with interconnected twins, such degradation can propagate outward, reducing the reliability of the broader SoTS. Addressing this issue requires mechanisms that allow DT-based services to operate coherently even when data becomes temporarily unavailable.

Because they maintain a continuously updated representation of their physical systems, they can forecast expected behaviour, interpret system state during observation gaps, and quantify confidence in these estimates. To make effective use of these capabilities within event-driven reasoning, they must be incorporated into the CEP pipeline through a formal mechanism. This thesis introduces an architecture that extends the role of DTs within the reasoning process by integrating their forecasting outputs directly into the event stream. The architecture provides a formal interface through which a DT may supply forecasted values when observations are missing. It detects gaps, invokes or queries the appropriate forecasting model, and inserts reconstructed events, annotated with their associated confidence, into the data flow. This enables continued operation during periods of missing data and supports adjustments in behaviour when confidence is low, until normal operating conditions resume.

\section*{Research Contributions}

This thesis advances the reliable operation of systems of twinned systems through four main contributions:

\textbf{Contribution 1 – Characterizing the Class of Systems of Twinned Systems (Chapter~\ref{ch:background})}  
Introduces SoTS as a distinct class that extends SoS through DTs, where the composition of virtualized constituents enables flexible coordination among distributed physical systems. The definition establishes a conceptual and structural foundation for understanding the organization, composition, and behaviour of SoTS.

\textbf{Contribution 2 – Analyzing the State of the Art in Systems of Twinned Systems (Chapter~\ref{ch:literature-review})}  
Conducts a systematic study of the state of the art in SoTS, including its conceptual foundations, architectural approaches, and methods of development. The synthesis consolidates current knowledge on how these systems are structured and operated, emphasizing the role of reliable operation in SoTS environments.

\textbf{Contribution 3 – Creating an Architecture for Reliable Operation in Systems of Twinned Systems (Chapter~\ref{ch:architecture})}  
Develops an architecture that maintains SoTS operation during periods of temporary data unavailability. The design introduces a standardized preprocessing layer with an interface through which DTs provide reconstructed values with confidence, while the architecture detects data gaps and integrates these annotated values into the processing pipeline to support continued system operation.

\textbf{Contribution 4 – Evaluating the Reliable SoTS Architecture within a Complex Event Processing Pipeline (Chapter~\ref{ch:results})}  
Implements and integrates the proposed architecture within a complex event processing pipeline to evaluate its performance under periods of temporary data unavailability. Experiments simulate constituent disconnections, with performance assessed through reconstruction accuracy and event-detection correctness relative to a baseline configuration.

Together, these contributions establish a theoretical foundation and a practical implementation framework for reliable operation in systems of twinned systems.
