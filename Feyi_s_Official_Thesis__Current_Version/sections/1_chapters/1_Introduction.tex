\chapter{Introduction}\label{ch:introduction}
% Big Picture
According to the International Council on Systems Engineering (INCOSE) Vision 2035 \cite{incose2035}, the growing interconnectivity of systems and the drive toward autonomous operation are contributing to a sharp rise in system complexity. Governments are acknowledging this shift. In Canada, the Improving the Reliability and Resilience of Canada’s Digital Infrastructure report~ \cite{ISED_DigitalInfrastructure} calls for reinforcing national data and communication systems to meet rising connectivity and information demands.
% At the same time, the global increase in development of cyber-physical systems is generating unprecedented data volumes across domains, reshaping how information is collected, processed, secured, and utilized \cite{atat2018big}.

This growing dependence on interconnected digital infrastructure parallels the global transition toward Industry 4.0 \cite{suleiman2022industry}, where digital transformation has become a central driver of innovation across sectors \cite{vial2021understanding}. Enterprises now integrate cyber-physical systems (CPS), which are systems that tightly couple computation, communication, and physical processes through continuous feedback loops. In CPS, embedded sensors and actuators enable digital controllers to monitor and influence physical behaviour in real time, forming the foundation of intelligent and autonomous infrastructure. These capabilities are further extended by Internet of Things (IoT) connectivity, artificial intelligence (AI), and automation technologies, which enhance scalability, adaptability, and decision-making capacity \cite{piardi2024role, hu2024industrial}. In such environments, information and analytics are now embedded within the control logic of physical system, making data have a direct influence on system behaviour and performance.

The integration of digital intelligence into physical processes is reshaping both established and emerging sectors. In smart cities, transportation, and utilities, cyber-physical systems coordinate sensing, communication, and actuation to achieve goals such as traffic optimization, grid balancing, or environmental monitoring \cite{tricomi2024smart, chowdhury2024data}. Similar transformations occur in domains that have historically relied on manual or physical control: precision agriculture uses sensor-driven feedback loops to optimize irrigation and fertilizer application \cite{chithrakumar2024revolutionizing}, while healthcare uses networked devices and AI-driven diagnostics to personalize treatment and monitor patient well-being \cite{manikandan2024significance}.

Across all domains, the availability, quality, and continuity of data directly influence system performance and decision-making. This growing dependency marks a fundamental shift. Modern enterprises increasingly rely on algorithmic reasoning to interpret and act upon information \cite{david2024infonomics}. As the boundaries between physical and digital operations dissolve, ecosystems of interconnected, data-driven cyber-physical systems are emerging.


% New Class of Systems
A major enabler of the digital transformation shaping modern enterprises is the emergence of digital twins (DTs). DTs serve as high-fidelity virtual counterparts of physical entities, continuously updated through sensor data and computational models to mirror their real-time state and behaviour \cite{kritzinger2018digital, leng2021digital}. They enable systems to be monitored, simulated, and optimized throughout their lifecycles, aligning with the goals of Industry~4.0 through data-informed decision-making and adaptive control \cite{pires2019digital}. Through DTs, integration and semantic consistency between the physical and digital domains are achieved, enabling logical interpretation, representation, and interaction across both \cite{zhang2024digital}.

While DTs enable the development of complex and autonomous cyber-physical systems, their true potential emerges when they interconnect. In practice, multiple DTs often coexist within shared environments, exchanging information, coordinating actions, and influencing one another’s behaviour \cite{dietz2020digital}. Together, these interactions give rise to collaborative networks of twinned systems. This collective behaviour reflects the system of Systems (SoS) paradigm, in which autonomous entities cooperate to achieve objectives that exceed their individual purposes \cite{maier1998architecting}.

SoS are characterized by several recurring traits. They exhibit constituent independence, where each subsystem can operate and evolve autonomously while contributing to shared goals. They demonstrate evolutionary development, as their composition and configuration change dynamically when new systems join, existing ones depart, or interconnections are restructured. They also display emergent behaviour, arising from the interactions among heterogeneous constituents that produce outcomes not predictable from the behaviour of individual systems \cite{nielsen2015systems}.

As networks of digital twins scale and become increasingly interconnected, they give rise to a new class of systems: Systems of Twinned Systems (SoTS). SoTS unite the high-fidelity modelling and state awareness of DTs with the organizational dynamics of SoS. This convergence supports enhanced situational awareness, coordinated decision-making, and adaptive operation across distributed, heterogeneous environments.


% Problem
SoTS are inherently event-driven environments. Each constituent digital twin continuously collects data from physical processes or other digital sources, such as sensors, analytical models, or neighbouring twins. From these inputs, it produces event streams that represent discrete observations or state changes within its operational context \cite{cugola2012processing}. These streams collectively form the dynamic information layer of the SoTS, where coordinated reasoning and emergent behaviour arise from the continuous interpretation of events across interconnected constituents.

Complex Event Processing (CEP)~\cite{luckham2002power} aligns naturally with this paradigm. CEP frameworks analyse sequences of atomic events to identify higher-level or composite patterns that represent meaningful situations or emergent phenomena \cite{cugola2012processing}. Within SoTS, CEP enables the detection of collective conditions that span multiple twins, providing a computational mechanism for higher-order reasoning and adaptive coordination.

In practice, however, most CEP architectures assume deterministic and complete data streams \cite{akila2016complex}. These assumptions rarely hold in real SoTS deployments. Sensors and communication links may introduce latency, noise, or missing data \cite{belgacem2025toward}, and digital twins may temporarily disconnect, reconfigure, or join dynamically, altering the structure and continuity of event flows \cite{fang2020improving}. Under such conditions, the flow of events can be disrupted, interrupting analysis and causing higher-level detections to fail or become inconsistent.

To ensure dependable operation, CEP frameworks within SoTS must move beyond the assumption of perfect data delivery. In dependability theory, fault tolerance combines error detection with system recovery and is often achieved through compensation. Compensation refers to the masking or mitigation of transient faults to sustain service continuity \cite{avizienis2004basic}. Reliability in SoTS therefore requires maintaining meaningful processing during temporary data loss. A reliable SoTS should reconstruct partial information, preserve analytical consistency, and restore normal operation once inputs recover. In this sense, compensation represents a core mechanism of reliability, enabling the system to tolerate temporary faults and continue delivering trustworthy event analysis under deteriorated conditions.

\section{Research Goals}
\label{sec:intro-research-questions}

This research aims to enhance the reliability of SoTS, focusing on how dependable operation can be sustained despite transient data disruptions and reconfiguration. 
While reliability is widely acknowledged as a key non-functional requirement for distributed systems, current SoTS research offers limited insight into how reliable behaviour is systematically achieved or maintained in practice. 

Given that SoTS are inherently event-driven environments, reliability at the system level is closely tied to the dependable processing of event streams exchanged among constituent twins. 
CEP naturally supports this paradigm by enabling the detection of higher-level, emergent situations from distributed event flows. 
However, conventional CEP architectures assume deterministic and complete data streams, conditions rarely satisfied in dynamic SoTS deployments. 
This motivates the development of a reliability-enhanced CEP framework capable of compensating for temporary disruptions while maintaining reasoning.

The research follows a structured progression through three key contributions, each defined by specific objectives and guided by focused research questions.

\vspace{1em}

\noindent\textbf{Contribution 1 – Characterize the State of the Art in Reliable SoTS Development}

\noindent\textbf{Objective:} Analyze existing SoTS literature to determine how reliability is currently defined, addressed, and supported in practice.

\begin{quote}
    \textit{RQ1: What is the current state of the art in Systems of Twinned Systems research in terms of architectural mechanisms, methods, and technologies? How do existing studies address the development of reliable SoTS?}
\end{quote}

This question establishes a foundation for understanding the current landscape of SoTS research by synthesizing its conceptual, architectural, and technological characteristics. It investigates how reliability is defined, interpreted, and operationalized across studies, revealing both the degree of maturity in current approaches and the existing research gaps motivating this work.

\vspace{1em}

\noindent\textbf{Contribution 2 – Derive Requirements for Reliable Event Processing in SoTS}

\noindent\textbf{Objective:} Analyze reliability-related findings from the literature to derive architectural requirements for dependable event processing in SoTS.

\begin{quote}
    \textit{RQ2: What reliability challenges arise in SoTS, and what architectural requirements can be derived to ensure reasoning continuity and correctness under temporary faults or data disruptions?}
\end{quote}

This question bridges the literature review and the architecture design stages.
It identifies key reliability challenges and extracts design requirements for enhancing event processing mechanisms that can maintain reliable reasoning under such conditions.

\vspace{1em}

\noindent\textbf{Contribution 3 – Design and Evaluate a Reliability-Enhanced CEP Architecture}

\noindent\textbf{Objective:} Develop an augmented Complex Event Processing architecture that applies reliability principles to sustain event reasoning continuity during temporary disruptions.

\begin{quote}
    \textit{RQ3: How can compensation-based mechanisms be integrated within event processing to maintain reliable event reasoning during transient data loss or reconfiguration?}
\end{quote}

This question focuses on the architectural design stage.
It explores how reliability mechanisms can be incorporated into CEP to compensate for missing or delayed event streams, ensuring operational continuity and correctness.

\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent\textbf{Objective:} Experimentally evaluate the proposed reliability-enhanced CEP architecture to quantify improvements in reliable event processing.

\begin{quote}
    \textit{RQ4: How effectively does the proposed CEP architecture maintain reliable event processing under varying fault conditions compared to baseline CEP systems?}
\end{quote}

This question assesses the performance of the proposed architecture under controlled fault scenarios. The results demonstrate the extent to which the reliability-oriented augmentation improves CEP dependability in SoTS-like environments.

\vspace{1em}


\paragraph{Thesis Structure.} 
The remainder of this thesis is organized as follows. 
\chref{ch:background} establishes the conceptual foundations, introducing Digital Twins, Systems of Systems, and Systems of Twinned Systems, along with key dependability and event-processing principles. 
\chref{ch:literature-review} characterizes the state of SoTS research, analyzing how reliability is addressed and identifying gaps motivating the need for enhanced event processing. 
\chref{ch:architecture} presents the proposed reliability-enhanced Complex Event Processing architecture, describing its components, data flow, and design rationale. 
\chref{ch:results} evaluates the architecture against a baseline CEP system under varying fault conditions. 
Finally, \chref{ch:discussion} interprets the results, and \chref{ch:conclusion} summarizes the thesis contributions, limitations, and directions for future research.
