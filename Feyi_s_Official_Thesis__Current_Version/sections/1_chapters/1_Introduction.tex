\chapter{Introduction}\label{ch:introduction}
The growing interconnection of systems and the increasing use of automation are contributing to a sharp rise in system complexity \cite{incose2035}, both across and within individual systems. At a system level, independently developed systems are increasingly engineered to cooperate so they can achieve objectives that exceed their individual capabilities. Such arrangements are described as systems of systems (SoS): large, distributed collections of autonomous systems that collaborate toward higher-level goals while retaining independent operation \cite{maier1998architecting}. Because SoS constituents join, leave, or reorganize in response to changing objectives, their collective behaviour evolves over time and gives rise to outcomes that individual systems cannot achieve alone \cite{nielsen2015systems}.
Within each constituent system, internal complexity is also increasing as physical systems are paired with digital representations that continuously measure, compute, and adjust behaviour. Digital twins (DTs) embody this integration. A DT is a digital representation of a physical system that mirrors its real-time state through continuous data exchange \cite{kritzinger2018digital}. This connection provides awareness of current conditions \cite{pires2019digital,zhang2024digital} and supports analysis through models that interpret or forecast behaviour. It also enables adaptation, where insights from the digital representation guide actions in the physical system \cite{leng2021digital}. As digital twins become more common, they create opportunities for coordinated monitoring and control across interconnected physical systems through their digital layers.

The convergence of system-level coordination found in SoS and the internal digital representation provided by DTs gives rise to a new class of systems referred to in this thesis as \textbf{systems of twinned systems (SoTS)}. These systems can be defined as follows:

\begin{definitionframe}
A system of twinned systems comprises digitally twinned systems organized according to system-of-systems principles, in which digital twins act as autonomous constituents that collaborate to achieve collective goals.
\end{definitionframe}

SoTS extend SoS coordination into the digital domain. Through their digital twins, systems can be composed and coordinated virtually while retaining control over their physical counterparts. This arrangement supports coordinated behaviour across distributed and heterogeneous environments, without requiring additional physical integration among systems.


A defining characteristic of SoTS is their reliance on continuous information exchange among digital twins. Their operation depends on consuming large volumes of timely data, yet their highly dynamic nature makes this difficult to sustain. As participants join, leave, or change roles, the structure and timing of data exchange shift accordingly, creating periods of temporary data unavailability that challenge the system’s ability to maintain correct and continuous service. Reliability, as defined by \citet{avizienis2004basic}, requires both correct behaviour and uninterrupted service delivery. SoTS face challenges on both dimensions. At the sensing and communication layers, latency, drift, bandwidth variation, and intermittent faults can interrupt the arrival of observations \cite{belgacem2025toward}. At the system level, dynamic reconfiguration alters information flows and their timing \cite{fang2020improving}. Without mechanisms to compensate for these disruptions, gaps in data can propagate through the system \cite{hanmer2013patterns} and impair SoTS operation.

These limitations motivate the central question of this thesis:
\textit{How can systems of twinned systems maintain reliable operation when data from the sources they depend on becomes temporarily unavailable?}

To address this question, the thesis begins with the challenge faced by the reasoning mechanisms that operate over SoTS data streams. This work adopts complex event processing (CEP) as the reasoning model because SoTS operate as event-driven systems whose behaviour emerges from the continuous generation, exchange, and interpretation of events among their digital twins. An event is a discrete observation or state change detected within a system, such as a sensor reading \cite{cugola2012processing}. CEP techniques detect meaningful patterns by processing sequences of such atomic events \cite{luckham2002power}. However, conventional CEP assumes uninterrupted streams of observations. Its treatment of uncertainty is limited to events that do arrive \cite{alevizos2017probabilistic}: it does not infer missing-but-expected events, and it does not maintain a synchronized model of the underlying system. As a result, CEP pipelines are sensitive to the temporary data unavailability that arises in SoTS.

Digital twins possess capabilities that conventional CEP lacks. Because they maintain a continuously updated representation of their physical systems, they can forecast expected behaviour, interpret state during observation gaps, and quantify confidence in their estimates. To make effective use of these capabilities within event-driven reasoning, they must be incorporated into the complex event processing pipeline through a formal mechanism. This thesis introduces an architecture designed to extend the role of digital twins within the reasoning process by integrating their forecasting capabilities directly into the event stream. The architecture provides a formal interface through which digital twins may supply forecasted values when observations are missing. It detects gaps, applies or requests the appropriate forecasting model, and inserts reconstructed values, together with their associated confidence, into the data flow. By marking both measured and estimated inputs, downstream CEP components can distinguish between them and interpret confidence levels when drawing inferences. This enables continued operation during periods of missing data and supports adjustments in behaviour when confidence is low, until normal conditions resume. The architecture is evaluated in a traffic simulation scenario (TBD), in which a digital twin applies a Kalman filter to generate probabilistic forecasts for reconstruction during periods of simulated data unavailability.

\section*{Research Contributions}

This thesis advances the reliable operation of systems of twinned systems through four main contributions that collectively define, analyze, design, and evaluate an implemented solution.

\textbf{Contribution 1 – Characterizing the Class of Systems of Twinned Systems (Chapter~\ref{ch:background})}
Introduces SoTS as a distinct class that extends SoS through DTs, where the composition of virtualized constituents enables flexible coordination among distributed physical systems.
The definition establishes a conceptual and structural foundation for understanding the organization, composition, and behaviour of SoTS.

\textbf{Contribution 2 – Analyzing the State of the Art in Systems of Twinned Systems (Chapter~\ref{ch:literature-review})}
Conducts a systematic study of the state of the art in SoTS, encompassing their conceptual foundations, architectural approaches, and methods of development. The synthesis consolidates current knowledge on how these systems are structured and operated, emphasizing the role of reliable operation in SoTS environments.

\textbf{Contribution 3 – Creating an Architecture for Reliable Operation in Systems of Twinned Systems (Chapter~\ref{ch:architecture})}
Develops an architecture that maintains SoTS operation during periods of temporary data unavailability. The design introduces a standardized preprocessing layer with an interface through which digital twins provide reconstructed values with confidence, while the architecture detects data gaps and integrates these annotated values into the processing pipeline to support continued system operation.

\textbf{Contribution 4 – Evaluating the Reliable SoTS Architecture within a Complex Event Processing Pipeline (Chapter~\ref{ch:results})}
Implements and integrates the proposed architecture within a complex event processing pipeline to evaluate its performance under periods of temporary data unavailability. Experiments simulate constituent disconnections representative of reconfiguration behaviour in SoTS, with performance assessed through reconstruction accuracy and event-detection correctness relative to a baseline configuration.
